# ALBION — CANON BIBLE
Version 0.1 · 19 October 2026 · Working reference for the fan-made digital army book

> **IMPORTANT:** Albion is the creator's original fan-made Warhammer Fantasy army concept. This file preserves existing project decisions; it is **not** permission to invent missing lore. The creator has final authority over canon.

## Status key
- **ESTABLISHED**: repeatedly specified or clearly accepted in previous creative work.
- **DEVELOPING**: an idea explored or described but with details unsettled.
- **UNKNOWN**: leave as a visible editorial placeholder; ask the creator before publishing.
- **REJECTED / DON'T INFER**: prevents plausible-sounding but incorrect inventions.

## Editorial contract for Claude
1. Do not invent named characters, invented place names, battles, birth dates, wars, dynasties, unit statistics, rules or quotations.
2. Do not replace the established roster with generic fantasy heroes. In particular, **Gwyth Antlerborn, Maddoc Greystone and the Hag of Nine Teeth** were fabricated as page filler and are **not established canon**. Do not present them as canon.
3. Content supplied here is a **lore reference**, not a requirement that every fact appear on the public-facing website. Select detail according to page purpose and tone.
4. For uncertain lore, write `[CANON TO CONFIRM: …]` in drafts. Never silently promote developing ideas into facts.
5. Respect the designed visual system: **1920×1080 stage; two-page spread; two text columns per page**, except for chapter openers, major images and deliberately special pages.
6. Never rasterize substantial body text into the illustration. Artwork and browser-set typography are separate.
7. Name the project as an **unofficial fan-made fictional army with AI-generated graphics** and avoid suggesting Games Workshop endorsement.

# 1. The island, its history, and its magic

### The ancient sequence — ESTABLISHED
- The **Old Ones** built an island-wide system of **pylons**, technological/magical infrastructure intended to hold back Chaos and protect Albion.
- The Old Ones departed. **Ancients** remained as long-lived/humanoid custodians. The **Fimir** arrived, and **humans** came later.
- **Argant**, the human founding/conquering king, drove the Fimir towards the salt coasts. The exact historical details and their mythical embellishments must not be confused.
- The pylons continue to function. Humans inherit systems they do not fully understand; maintenance knowledge survives through druidic craft, ritual and arcana — degraded engineering preserved as tradition and practical magic.
- **Argant is not the separate Sleeping/Returning King.** The two must not be collapsed into one individual.

### Pylons — ESTABLISHED
- Massive original works sometimes retain visible runic 'wiring'; many smaller/older sites have been absorbed into **barrows, witch huts, caves, sheep folds, standing stones, hill forts and ruined masonry**.
- Wolves often den around old pylon sites, a mundane recurring association.
- A minor or misaligned pylon can become a weak **Beastman ingress point**.
- Ancients retain special responsibility for the system; many human religious interpretations misunderstand its true origin.

### The Ancients — ESTABLISHED with DEVELOPING detail
- Humanoid old custodians, not identical to the Old Ones. They are not all benevolent or mentally intact.
- Some are asleep, eccentric, or damaged by age. An erratic **Trickster Ancient** is associated with impossible knots, road junctions, gifts, and eventually roundabouts.
- **Snakespeaker / Sarfareth** is an Ancient antagonist tied to the old system and to Argant. **Name relationship needs creator confirmation**: prior discussions also use Sarfareth as a Rememberer called 'He Who Speaks of Snakes'. Do not combine or separate these identities further without checking.
- The larger conflict includes keeping the machinery working versus the modern desire to understand or control it.

### Religion and politics — ESTABLISHED
- Albion has a **High Church** and older folk faith; saintly and divine interpretations of Argant and the distant past coexist with the practical workings of the pylons.
- Round shields are called **Argant round shields**; shield backs may be used by commanders for planning. Reformed stone churches sometimes use round tables.
- The lore should remain socially textured: traditions, institutions, politics, superstitions and inherited routine matter as much as battles.

### Geography — ESTABLISHED at regional level; UNKNOWN in detail
- **Grey Coast**, **Forest and Fenn**, and broad Red/Blue areas of influence are established. Do not invent a definitive labelled map, settlements or boundaries without creator approval.
- Tone: ancient, wet, haunted, barrow-strewn, Bronze Age/Britonnic island.

# 2. Hosts / major factions

The four **hosts** are overlapping cultural/political/magical factions; do not treat them as four modern nation-states with fixed borders.

## BLUE HOST — ESTABLISHED
**Leader:** **Alfran Longbritches / Longshanks**, sometimes styled 'Shortsword'. Modernising, ambitious, foreign-influenced. Tartan trousers, high boots, moustache; a strong visual contrast with antiquarian Albion.

**Affiliated forces and imagery:** painted warriors, javelin troops, war dogs, **Waiting Guard**, javelin ram-riders, giant ally **Tam Loggins**. Alfran rides **Shaggy**, a Loch Ness-like creature, with a throne saddle.

**Court:** younger brother **Elfred 'Sidesaddle'**, linked to a **Court of Antlers** and a deer ridden sideways. **The Beast** is an unusually imposing guardian figure who came from an Araby prince's court. He has a stolen purple gemstone in his belly button, understands intricate court politics, and instinctively protects Elfred. Do not write the Beast as Elfred's father figure.

**Politics:** Alfran favours standardisation/reform, including roundabouts, and wishes to rationalise or make accountable the old sources of power. **DEVELOPING** how far he would push this.

## RED HOST — ESTABLISHED
**Leader:** **Maddoc the Flame**, a woman. **Not 'Maddoc Greystone'.**

**Forces and imagery:** fire breathers, ember druids, painted women slingers, fire maidens, boar chariots, **Shrikers** (banshee-like women with long polearms and fire-linked visual language), and a spectacular bronze chariot drawn by **she-lions**. A **Bloodmaiden / Bloodflame sorceress** rides an ox or boar cart (precise version DEVELOPING).

**Tone:** fire, spectacle, charged ritual, heat, aggression — but not generic evil fire cult.

## GREEN HOST — ESTABLISHED
**Region:** **Forest and Fenn**; frequently treated as Albion's default earthy green world.

**Characters:** **Robin Fair**, outlaw/folk-king; **Big John**, a very tall, gentle bodyguard; fenn witches and hags; Robin's son **Robinson** (later Earl — exact sequence to confirm).

**Forces and imagery:** **Dykejumpers**, **Robin Fair woodsmen** wearing paper fox masks, short willow bows, reed boats, bog imps, will-o'-wisps, casters referred to as **Root and Knots**.

**Robin Fair:** famously handsome, cheeky, prehistoric clothing rather than medieval armour, armed with a famous **nine-inch bronze blade**; refuses horses. Vegetarianism is jokingly blamed for his unexceptional height. His exact role guarding a fenn witch/sleeping king should be checked before use as published prose.

**Witches:** **Jenny Willows** (or **Jenynn** in some notes; exact spelling needs confirmation): boat, lantern, smoky wisps, extraordinarily long oar-arm. **Knot Wife** carries an impossible-knots cane and has a frog familiar.

## GREY HOST — ESTABLISHED
**Nature:** the older supernatural Albion: coast, barrows, weather, drowned, sleeping and undying things.

**Characters and creatures:** **Old Shannok** (blind white-eyed wizard, long nails, supported on a moving throne of stones joined by vines, stones suspended but not rotating); the **Wild Hunt** and its undying Sutton Hoo-like king; giants; coast hags, kelpies, drowned **Waiting Guard**, black hounds, Ancients.

**Wild Hunt:** boat-chariot; retinue includes wife, wizard, crone and two demi-giant sons; beasts include lion, stag, unicorn and boar. Its horn is later stolen by Longshanks (the timeframe/details are DEVELOPING).

**Important:** do not represent every Grey figure as automatically undead or evil.

# 3. Named characters and creatures reference

## Core characters — ESTABLISHED
- **Argant:** conquering/founding human king; associated with the repulsion of the Fimir, religious legacy and round shields. Traditions give him a wife, mother-in-law, druid and two sons; details DEVELOPING.
- **Old Shannok:** ancient blind wizard, floating-stone throne (stones suspended, not revolving).
- **Alfran Longbritches:** Blue reformer; sometimes Longshanks / Shortsword.
- **Elfred Sidesaddle:** younger brother of Alfran; softer and decorative personality; antler court.
- **The Beast of Alfran's court:** guardian, former Araby-court dependent; purple belly jewel.
- **Maddoc the Flame:** female leader of Red Host.
- **Robin Fair:** Green outlaw/folk-king; bronze knife and fox-mask aesthetic.
- **Big John:** Robin's unusually tall, gentle protector.
- **Old Gul ('White Gull'):** egg-throwing witch, seagull cloak, can summon waves.
- **Knot Wife:** hag with impossible-knots cane and frog familiar.
- **Big Gul:** a creature combining roughly 70% albatross, 20% dragon, 10% ancient god — huge wings, long lean body, hooked beak, brutal talons, scaled throat and legs, sparse heavy jewellery and an intelligent eye.
- **Tin Teeth:** wyvern with tin markings/streaks, flat head, digging and flight, nose ring; adults blind, young fly.
- **Tim and Tom:** father **Big Pete**.
- **Stubby:** Saltman and captain in Albion's rugby-like **Saltwater Cup**, seventh son of a seventh son, father of six boys. His 'Stubby' nickname is accepted happily, not a psychological wound.

## Hags and druids — ESTABLISHED / spelling subject to editorial confirmation
The named hags have included **Mother Hearth**, **Genny Green Teeth**, **Old Nag**, **Annie Lantern**, **Jenynn Skratch**. Past notes also vary some names, including Old Neg/Nag; preserve the latest agreed spelling only after confirmation.

A druid typically works as a trio: **druid, novice, keeper**, with the keeper often a protector/bodyguard. Novices are conventionally **gelded**, making Old Nag's **Robinson novice** particularly unusual. Druidic practices include fire, rain and stone-moving.

## Giants and monsters — ESTABLISHED
- Giants include **Dunce Peter**, **Eddard the Dunn** and **Tam Loggins**.
- **Kelpies** look like seaweed horses fused with their riders.
- **Black hounds**, drowned warriors, will-o'-wisps and the Wild Hunt are recurring.
- Fomorian/Fimir units include one-eyed fishlike warriors, musicians/standards/leaders and the **Picked-Over King** carried by retainers; exact unit roster still developing.

# 4. Fimir society — established developments

- Fimir have broods and **brood mothers**. With age, brood mothers become increasingly magical; in origin, magic grows from maintaining the **small hearth**, a lifetime of ordinary skill so deep it turns supernatural.
- **Failed broods** can result in **Skallies**, bleak ghoul-like itinerants: flint knives, stones, hunger. They eat eggs. Do not romanticise them as trinket collectors or sophisticated cooks.
- Music can sometimes hold Skallies together on a primal level, but they are otherwise desperate and difficult to help.
- Age drives social precedence: who's older matters. Birthdays and giving presents on important days matter. Rival broods can feud and rarely appeal to a queen to settle disputes.
- Flint weaponry is a distinctive material culture. A special relic is a **jewel-encrusted lidless eye** kept wet with brackish water by an attending witch. The description of its cosmic origin remains DEVELOPING.
- Keep their society stranger, sadder and more distinctive than generic swamp goblins.

# 5. Araby, trade and social detail

### Araby — ESTABLISHED
- Albions associate Araby with extraordinary wealth, courts, luxury, harems, pillows, tigers, lions, giant snails, magical objects and jewellery.
- **Ring-money:** 1 gold ring is the base unit; **5 rings = 1 amulet**; **13 amulets = 1 crown = 65 rings**. Under the spectacle is a serious monetary system with standardized value and accounting implications.
- Alfran has foreign intellectual influences; do not turn all imported ideas into jokes or characterize Araby as intellectually primitive.

### Everyday customs — ESTABLISHED
- **Blackweed** is used rather like tea, chewing tobacco and a mild intoxicant: brewed, chewed or smoked.
- Social detail (sport, minor titles, gestures, craft traditions, coinage, presents, food) is part of the world, not padding.

# 6. Visual canon and miniatures

- Strong, chunky, immediately readable miniatures; **Bronze Age/Britonnic** material culture with hints of barrows and Sutton Hoo.
- Reduce gratuitous detail by 20–50%; avoid generic high-medieval full plate armour. Aim for about **20% odd or funny**, presented seriously.
- Painted character and monster plates, miniature-style showcases, ink line art, marginal drawings and diagrams should all coexist.
- An army book in a **6th-edition-era Warhammer Fantasy editorial spirit**; avoid directly copying any official page or artwork.
- Default interior: **two columns per page**, four per spread; art may interrupt columns. Use a full spread where justified.

# 8. Anti-hallucination examples

**Wrong:** 'Maddoc Greystone, famed warchief of the Red Host, slew the Hag of Nine Teeth at Blackwater Ford.'

**Right:** 'Maddoc the Flame leads the Red Host.' If there is no known named battle, use `[CANON TO CONFIRM: notable battle]`.

**Wrong:** 'Gwyth Antlerborn is Albion's greatest hero.'

**Right:** The current known hero list includes Robin Fair, Alfran Longbritches, Maddoc the Flame and others listed here. Do not invent a substitute.

**Wrong:** Give made-up numerical combat profiles as if canonical.

**Right:** Use `Profiles and points: awaiting design approval` unless an approved rules source is supplied.
