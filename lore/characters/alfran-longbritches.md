# Alfran Longbritches

## Heroic style

**Lord Excalibar of the Blaewen Ridings** is Alfran's grand heroic style, associated with his possession of the Sword in the Stone, which he could not draw and therefore carried away with the stone itself. His ordinary name remains **Alfran Longbritches**.

## Faction name

**The Blaewen Ridings** is the chosen name for Alfran's Blue-aligned lands and host, replacing the provisional label **Blue Host** in new descriptive writing. The name evokes the territories and divisions of his growing earldom-based realm. Older Blue Host references remain useful as a working cross-reference.

## Summary

Alfran Longbritches is a prominent leader of Albion's **Blue Host** and an advocate of reform and modernisation. His ambitions and foreign influences put him in tension with Albion's older customs and institutions, but he is not intended to be a simple villain.

## Established character and visual details

- Lean build, with a moustache, tartan trousers and high boots.
- His visual design draws on a modern, self-consciously polished image against Albion's older Bronze Age world; avoid generic medieval plate armour.
- He rides **Shaggy**, a Loch Ness-like creature that can carry his throne.
- He possesses **the horn that can summon the Waiting Guard**, ancient warriors drowned and preserved in Albion's bogs. The Guard are a sleeping or waiting army awaiting **Argant**, Albion's founding king, rather than Alfran. **Alfran can summon and field them in battle with the horn; they are a playable Special unit.**
- He is the elder brother of **Elfred 'Sidesaddle'**.
- The **Beast** is a figure at Alfran's Blue Court, with a particularly protective relationship towards Elfred.
- He is influenced by ideas from abroad, including Araby's sophistication around money; the exact extent of each influence needs further editorial definition.
- His modernising instinct extends to standardising road junctions and roundabouts, which have folkloric significance in Albion.

## Mother — the northern Empress

Alfran and Elfred's mother comes from the **petty earldoms of the North**. She is the **third daughter of a Duke**, and had no meaningful expectation of inheriting a lordship in her own right. Her heraldry claims **seventeen northern dukedoms**, represented by seventeen flags; this is distinct from the little Albion tribes she **restyles as Earldoms**. By elevating those tribes' titles beneath her, she **styles herself Empress**. Her imperial status is thus a self-fashioned claim, not an inherited imperial throne. The exact basis of each northern ducal claim remains open. Some of the newly styled Albion Earldoms are **as small as ten-acre meadows**. **Alfran simply lets his mother get on with her imperial styling**, neither objecting nor treating it as a serious issue. She appears lavishly enthroned amid many banners on a vast wheeled carriage, wearing furs and a crown, with attendants and a caged bird. **She has her own Royal Guard**, distinct from Alfran's ordinary forces, **styled in the fashion of her northern homeland**. Her Royal Guard are **The Seventeen Standards — Winter Household Regiment**. These are **real fighting household troops**, not merely ceremonial attendants: the household force includes spear-and-shield warriors, a swordsman, an archer, standard-bearers and household figures including a falconer and a handler with a spotted great cat. Their look combines great patchwork heraldic banners, furs, blue/red/cream northern dress, helmets and round shields. The identity of the recruits and precise battlefield rules remain open. Their name and standard count are **seventeen**, matching the Empress's seventeen ducal flags.

## Magical supporters and opposition

Druids **do resist Alfran's looting of ancient barrows**, but his wealth allows him to **pay off selected individuals**; this does not imply general approval from the druidic establishment. His magical entourage includes **one Great Druid** and **the Three**, a group of **three special Knotwitches**. The Great Druid is a **mad, eccentric old philosopher in the spirit of Diogenes**, the Greek philosopher associated with living in a large jar; the precise Albion version of his dwelling, personality and magic remains to be defined. The Three are a **maiden, mother and crone** who weave Alfran's deeds into **Bayeux Tapestry-like narrative textiles**. Their names, relationship to ordinary Knotwives and precise roles remain to be established.

## Old Berrick of the Barrel — provisional character development

**Old Berrick of the Barrel** is a *working name* for Alfran's Great Druid. A filthy, eccentric old philosopher with a Diogenes-like disregard for comfort and courtly rank, he lives near or sometimes inside a great clay jar by choice. **His philosophy goes no further than where his next meal is coming from**: practical hunger matters more to him than elaborate theories or lofty political ideals. He mocks Alfran's ambition and vanity, while Alfran values his magical skill and tolerates his insolence. Their mutual respect is uneasy: each sees the other as a necessary nuisance. A possible line: *“You may command the kingdom. You cannot command the rain.”* **All details in this paragraph are provisional inventions pending creator approval.**

## Barrow plunder and imperial wealth

Alfran **loots Albion's ancient burial barrows to finance his expanding empire**. He has **wagons of richly worked, Anglo-Saxon/Sutton Hoo-style gold** taken from the graves of the old kings and other buried dead. This treasure is a practical source of wealth for his campaigns, newly created earldoms and projects. His removal of the entire Sword in the Stone belongs to this broader habit of appropriating Albion's buried past.

## The Sword in the Stone

Alfran **could not draw the sword from the stone**, so he ordered **the stone itself removed and carried away, sword still embedded**. His ram-drawn wagon displays the whole object as a trophy. He thus possesses the sword-and-stone physically without having fulfilled the legendary feat of drawing the blade. The sword's precise original significance and who, if anyone, can draw it remain open.

## Enclosure and rural transformation

Alfran is transforming parts of the **Field and Fenn into kingdoms and lordships**, with **fences and walls dividing formerly shared meadows**. **The new lords are Alfran's supporters**, appointed to govern. **The change from Masters to Guilders is a consequence of the new order, not a conversion Alfran deliberately campaigns to encourage.** Under this system, traditional **Masters become Guilders**, shifting their position from independent smith-leaders toward **paid professionals employed directly by those who can afford them**, rather than serving communities through need and family ties. This benefits the new lords far more than the ordinary people whose established access and relationships are disrupted. His modernising project is therefore a major source of conflict with traditional rural communities, without necessarily making him a straightforward villain.

## Rapid creation of Earls

Alfran is in **growth mode**, creating **Earls wherever he can** as he extends his influence. Titles and territories are granted rapidly to supporters, expanding his system of lordship. **The new Earls are expected to raise troops for Alfran**, alongside their wider territorial and lordly obligations. This is active, opportunistic political expansion rather than a slow, settled hereditary order. The competence and exact responsibilities of individual new Earls vary or remain to be established.

## Connections

- **Blue Host:** Alfran's political and military sphere.
- **Elfred Sidesaddle:** his younger brother, associated with the Court of Antlers.
- **The Beast:** member of the Blue Court; formerly associated with an Arabian prince.
- **Shaggy:** his unusual mount and throne-bearer.
- **Tam Loggins:** giant allied with the Blue Host.

## Associated military themes

Blue Host imagery includes painted warriors, javelins, war dogs, the Waiting Guard and javelin ram riders. Their precise relationship to Alfran's personal command needs editorial checking rather than assumption.

## Art direction

Bronze Age Albion silhouette, tartan trousers, high boots, moustache, lean figure, no medieval plate. Emphasise the contrast between reforming confidence and a stubbornly archaic world. For the 6th-edition codex, commission both a painted portrait and a rough black-and-white ink sketch.

## The military consequences of the new earldoms
Alfran is effectively **introducing feudalism** to Albion. Newly appointed Earls are granted territory and status in return for raising and maintaining men under his authority. This sits uneasily beside older kinship and communal obligations. His paid **Plaid Men**, equipped more uniformly than ordinary Painted Warriors, reflect this transformation, without making Albion a conventional cavalry culture.

## The horn and its dead
Alfran's recovered **Horn of Argant** is a monumental relic supported by a dedicated bearer while a hero blows it. Its call raises the **Waiting Guard**, who are simply ancient bog-preserved zombies; they have no present consciousness, king-recognition or loyalty dilemma. Their connection to Argant belongs to their ancient origin, not to their current behaviour.
