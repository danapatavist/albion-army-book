# Druidic Culture

## Overview
Albion's druidry includes established colleges and rarer independent practitioners. Druids are distinct from **witches** and from the **Ancients**. Their practice is associated with magical knowledge, training and the stewardship of old pylon sites; the precise division of duties remains under development.

## College tradition — established
College-trained druids ordinarily work as an appointed **Druid, Novice and Sword** trio.

- The **Druid** is the senior spellcaster.
- The **Novice**, traditionally gelded, is an apprentice with limited personal magic. He principally supports the Druid; he may eventually become a full Druid.
- The **Sword** is an armed guardian appointed through the colleges and **sworn to that Druid for life**.

The Novice may eventually have a few petty spells independently, or contribute an extra casting roll, but these remain *unsettled gameplay proposals*. The Sword's exact wound-protection mechanics are also unsettled.

## Solitary druids — established
Solo druids are rarer than college trios. They may be self-taught, have left their cult or college, or have been expelled. Solitude does not itself establish criminality or hostility.

## The five colleges — material to reconcile
Previous development references **five major druidic centres** associated with old pylon complexes, training and maintenance. Recalled names include **Shannock's Pylon**, **Green Pylon**, **Grey Pylon** and **Worldhearth**; Worldhearth is associated with a prestigious women-only healing order. **Blue and Red** are described as newer, lesser pylons rather than two of the original five great centres.

A separate, earlier *proposal* for five Grey cave colleges used the labels **Tide, Stone, Wreck, Mist and Deep**. Do **not** silently equate this proposal with the five major pylon centres or declare all ten names canonical. The complete authoritative list, hierarchy and geography need confirmation.

## Magical practices and references
Established imagery across Albion includes fire and embers, rain and bowls of water, stone-moving, suspended stones and constructs, animal-associated seers, and pylon stewardship. These are **not confirmed as five formal college specialisations**.

## Dispute settlement and livelihood

A major everyday function of druids is **arbitrating disputes**, particularly between people or communities. Calling in a druid to settle a dispute is customary in Field and Fenn, and **fees for arbitration are a druid's main source of income**. Albion is a **respect-based society**: a druid's judgement carries weight through acknowledged standing and respect, rather than primarily through formal legal authority. The exact procedures and forms of payment are not yet specified.


## Social standing and wartime command

**Druids have higher status than witches**. **Druids technically outrank Masters (smith-leaders)** in formal status, although both are influential and **either may command an army**. The cultural contrast is often that **druids are learned, scholarly 'nerds' and Masters are physically formidable, practical 'jocks'**; formal precedence does not always translate into who leads people in a fight. A medium-sized Albion army **rarely takes the field without a druid**, even if its commander is a Master.

## Relationships
- **Witches** practise a distinct tradition and are generally cheaper tabletop magic choices. Their social organisation should not be assumed to mirror colleges.
- **Ancients** are surviving humanoids left by the Old Ones to maintain pylons. They are not ordinary druids.
- **Old Shannock** is an Ancient with a small retinue of druid warriors; these guardians are not therefore Ancients.
- **Old Nag's Robinson Novice** is unusual because Robinsons rarely serve as novices.

## Unresolved
The complete list of five great colleges, their jurisdiction and leadership, rites, the origin of the college/trio convention, and the relationship of the earlier Grey cave-college concepts to current lore.
