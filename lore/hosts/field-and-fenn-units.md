# Field and Fenn — Units and Creatures

The **Field and Fenn** are unincorporated field, woodland, marsh and swamp communities rather than a centrally commanded faction. “Green Host” is a provisional army-book classification, not their established political identity.

## Landscape and travel

The **Field and Fenn** is a patchwork of **swamp, river, dyke and meadow**, crossed in places by **plank roads**. Particularly among the **river folk**, people carry **sturdy, lightweight, shield-like one-person coracles**, constructed from **woven willow covered in hide**, so they can quickly take to the water when the route demands it. **These portable willow boats are distinct from the standard round fighting shields**. These are practical everyday travel equipment, not necessarily dedicated war boats.

## Livelihoods

Field and Fenn communities have **mixed livelihoods**: farming, fishing, rabbiting, poaching, baking and varied artisan crafts. They are not solely marsh fishers or farmers.

## Itinerant craftspeople

Travel between communities is driven less by a distinct class of traders than by **craftspeople following commissions and going wherever the work is**. There is **no strong guild system** regulating these trades: a craftsman's standing rests on **his word and proof of craft**—demonstrated workmanship and a reputation for keeping promises. A craftsman who breaks his word or produces shoddy work may be **driven out of the community**. The Big May is an important opportunity for artisans to find their next jobs; they then travel to the communities where those jobs are needed.

## Community boundaries and rivalry

Field and Fenn settlements are **distinct communities with recognised borders**. Neighbours have **disputes**, sometimes fight **wars**, and compete in **ball games** that can turn violent. They also hold other **competitions**. The Big May includes **sporting events and lotteries** as well as its trading, music and social arrangements. **The Big May** is a huge festival held at their **main mega-market**, drawing communities together for celebrations and contests. Above all it is a **trade fair**: artisans seek their next commissions, musicians perform, and families negotiate arrangements, potentially including **marriage exchanges in which a daughter is effectively sold to a family with a son**. Whether it has any religious significance is **not established**. The communities should not be presented as a unified peaceful polity; the precise customs, rules and causes of their rivalries remain open.

## Settling disputes

Rather than leaving disputes entirely to a village Master or informal group judgement, communities **usually call a druid to arbitrate**. Such paid dispute settlement is **a principal livelihood of druids**, indeed their main source of income. Albion is a **respect-based society**, so the authority of a druid's decision rests chiefly on the standing and respect accorded to them, rather than a central legal apparatus. Precise procedures and payments remain open.

## The May — visual reference

The private Green archive includes `green culture/May.png`, a concept plate captioned **“A May — The temporary city at harvest.”** It portrays a vast seasonal market settlement on waterways: boat moorings, raft-linked market channels, causeways, timber and reed tent inns, grassy pyramid mounds with chalk markings, and great broken pylon or gate ruins. These are visual design references, not automatic confirmation of every label or historical claim on the image. **The Big May is the harvest gathering depicted here**: despite the spring-sounding name, this is a great harvest-time festival and temporary market city. Another archive image, `green culture/Fennmen Bloodbowl.png`, depicts a fantastical, rough ball game, supporting the established sporting culture; specific pictured players and team roles are not automatically canon.

## Settlements and giant avoidance

Villages favour **raised meadows** amid the mixed waterways and low ground. Typical dwellings are **low turf-built homes**, akin to Icelandic turf houses, or **thatched A-frame buildings**. Both keep a low, landscape-blending profile: from a giant's elevated, top-down view, settlements are deliberately **unobtrusive**, avoiding the attention of passing giants. Roads and plankways are generally **four to five feet wide**. More grandiose architecture also exists, but is **usually built on pylons**. These buildings can serve **any purpose**—they are not restricted to religion, halls or residences. The Fenners hold **many different beliefs**, many of them centred on **water holes**, sacred both for the water itself and for what may lie beneath. Ancient Albion **drowned warriors**, and in the preserving bogs some remain as the **Waiting Guard**, **awaiting the return of Argant**, Albion's founding king, like a sleeping army. **Alfran possesses the horn that can call them**, although he is not the king they await.

## Shared Albion infantry principle

Across **every Albion host**, the bulk of ordinary troops are **Painted Warriors**, using the **same miniature box** and distinguished chiefly by paint scheme. Their equipment may be chosen in combinations of **round shield, pike, hand weapon, sling, or javelin**; precise tabletop options are not finalised. Albion is notable for having **few distinct infantry types** rather than extensive differentiated infantry ranges (whether this also implies a low infantry model count is not yet established). Hosts gain much of their identity from specialist troops, creatures, magic, leaders and how the common warriors are painted.

## Core army composition

The **bulk of a Green / Field and Fenn army** consists of **painted warriors** (the same miniature box used by Blue and Red armies, differentiated through painting rather than separate sculpts), **Low Arrows** (low-position archers using willow or reed screens), and **Dykers / Dykejumpers** (mobile polemen). These are its ordinary mainstay troops; the stranger creatures and war machines are supplementary rather than the army's backbone.

**Low Arrows are standard Core troops.** **Shield Wall is a basic unit/formation available to every Albion host**, not a Blue specialism. Its shields are **fairly standard round shields of an Anglo-Saxon type**, common across Albion. Painted Warriors are a shared Albion troop type. **Swords are rare**; ordinary warriors primarily carry **farm implements or ritual instruments** used as weapons. Precise loadouts and Shield Wall rules remain unsettled. Dykers' formal roster category has not yet been confirmed.

## Jack and his giantess

**Jack and his giantess fight as a paired character choice**: they come together, rather than being recruited separately, but **each has a separate miniature base**. **Jack's mother**, the bean-witch, is an **optional extra**, not automatically included, and uses **her own separate base**. No shared or combined base. The exact army-list cost and rules are still undecided.

## Robin Fair's companions

**Big John** always fights alongside **Robin Fair**. **The Bard** likewise always accompanies Robin's group, riding **on Big John's back** because the Bard's legs do not work. The Bard may be able to **swing his guitar for a small melee attack**, despite principally travelling on John's back; this is a tentative tabletop idea rather than a confirmed rule. Treat the three as linked companions rather than independent, freely deployed figures; their precise game mechanics remain to be determined.

## Robin Fair's masked woodsmen

**Robin Fair's woodsmen** are a **distinctive-model elite unit**, rather than simply standard Painted Warriors in a different paint scheme. They wear **a mixture of animal masks** (not all fox faces like Robin's) and evoke outlaw **men-at-arms or highwaymen**. They can be fielded as a regular elite unit and **Robin Fair can join them**; they are not exclusively his compulsory bodyguard. Their exact weapons, special rules and final unit name remain open.

## Known formations and creatures

- **Swamp Dragons** — simply **very large crocodiles** of the Field and Fenn swamps. Despite the name, they are not established as supernatural or fire-breathing dragons. They are **wild, not domesticated**; Dykemen steer them toward the enemy by prodding them with their poles. They do not truly control the animals. **The Dykemen stay alongside the crocodiles during battle**, continuing to prod and steer them. Their exact size and tabletop rules remain open.
- **Dykejumpers** (also labelled Fenn Dykers in concept art) — fast-moving polemen who flit, spin and vault with their poles. In proposed tabletop rules, bow attacks against them suffer a **−1 penalty** because they are hard to hit while moving. They have the fighting ability of ordinary men rather than elite combatants. A **second dagger** is a possible additional weapon, not yet settled. Precise rules wording and balance remain provisional.
- **Low Arrows** — archers who **crouch behind little walls of willow or reeds** and fire from that low position, making the archers harder to hit. Exact tabletop rules remain unsettled.

## War machines
- **[Honey Dragon](../artefacts/honey-dragon.md)** — a catapult hurling a jar of honey and hornets, furnished with willow wings and a dragon-like beak/nose for direction. Albion folk call machines **dragons**.

In ordinary life, Field and Fenn **war machines are principally anti-giant defences**, used to ward off giants rather than wage war against other people. **[Giants](../creatures/giants.md) are found throughout Albion**, not only in the Field and Fenn. They are generally nuisances that break down structures obstructing ancient **ley lines**.
- **[Blinding Brass](../artefacts/blinding-brass.md)** — directs light to blind giants and send them away.

## Local leadership

Communities are headed by **Masters**: beefy, entirely non-magical working bronze smiths who make and sharpen their people's weapons. They use a short working hammer and chain; a small anvil is a possible miniature accessory. Smithing is the highest craft, above carpentry. **Masters are generic, unnamed generals** who command Field and Fenn forces, not unique named special characters. **Masters can rally the extended-family households, and the households can call upon their smith in return**: this is a reciprocal relationship of service and obligation, rather than one-way feudal command. **This arrangement especially applies where there are no towns:** a single household cannot feed or support a full-time Master smith, so several scattered homesteads collectively sustain one smith, who serves them in return. It should not automatically be treated as the political structure of every settlement in Albion. Albion armies should offer ordinary customisable leaders and spellcasters alongside legendary named figures. Their exact points cost, character tier, leadership and combat rules are not yet decided.

## Rallying in war

When households need to fight or respond to a threat, **Masters take charge or are called upon by the households**. This follows the same reciprocal relationship that joins smiths and homesteads in peacetime; no formal election of a supreme commander is established.

When neighbouring communities face a greater threat, they may call on **a respected great figure such as Robin Fair**. A **civilisational threat** may bring the intervention of **an Ancient**, or **Diddy Mann may appear**. For any serious war, **finding a druid willing to support the campaign is important**. This is a scale of response rather than a rigid hierarchy or guaranteed chain of command.


**Druids technically outrank Masters**, but both are important and **either may lead an army**. In social character, learned, scholarly druids are the 'nerds' and physically formidable, practical smith-Masters the 'jocks'. A druid also has **higher status than a witch**. It is **rare for a medium-sized Albion army to go to war without a druid**.

## Magic

Witches and druids are distinct. Witches are generally cheaper choices in game terms. Druids normally appear as a trio: **Druid, Novice and Sword** (bodyguard). Solitary druids are very rare. No exact wound-allocation rule has been approved.

## Related figures

Robin Fair, John Acorn, Duck the Bard, Genny Willows, Diddy Mann and his woodwose, Old Shannock, Jack, his giantess and his vine-witch mother are connected to this society, **not** its collective rulers.

## Not established as real

**Bog Imps** are not confirmed creatures of Albion; the current preference is that they probably **do not exist**. Do not present them as an actual Field and Fenn unit or species.

## Folklore, not units

**Will-o'-the-wisps** are simply mysterious lights and the stories told about them, **not confirmed magical beings or creatures**. They are not a Field and Fenn troop type.

## Unresolved
